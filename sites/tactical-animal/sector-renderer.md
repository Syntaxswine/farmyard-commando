# Sector presentation

The field renders the shared `world-grid.js` camera window: at most nine 24 m blocks, shifted inward at the edges of the 240 m sector. Crossing one interior block seam keeps six existing block objects, releases three instance buffers, and builds three new blocks. Ground, opaque brick walls and cargo geometry are prepared before discovery. The simulation's seen/currently visible sets control their appearance; culling never modifies terrain, units, interactions, remembered cells or saved state.

Each operation owns one set of surface textures, wall/ground materials, a unit cube, cargo atlas textures and cached painted cargo prototypes. Blocks borrow those resources. Releasing a block disposes its instance buffers and sandbag sprite materials; operation teardown disposes the shared resources. Artwork loads once per operation, including original sandbags. Interactions share textures and geometry, create only resident leaves, and recover their authoritative saved open/inspection state on revisit. Actor rigs outside the window leave the scene; eight recent rigs may stay in an LRU cache, with older rigs disposed. Pending actor loads use cancellation tickets so a retired window cannot resurrect a stale model.

Camera pan, player follow and detected-opponent focus use world coordinates and clamp to playable bounds. Large-sector orthographic zoom is capped by the projected ground-plane extents inside the resident window. This keeps an 844×390 landscape view over loaded ground at the widest requested zoom, including just before/after a seam. The 13 m close phone framing and 6–35 m range on the two legacy maps are preserved. Seeing beyond the actual sector edge shows the normal field background.

Preparation and tactical maps scale to the full playable bounds. Preparation surveys terrain without plotting guards; the live minimap uses remembered terrain and currently detected enemies only. The checkpoint's brick/window edges retain the adapter's conservative opaque, 2 m wall representation. Window openings are not cut into these walls in this milestone. The red painted barrel is static cover; this import does not add explosive-barrel gameplay. Sandbags use the original source painting within the supplied one-tile footprint and cover height.

Flame aiming and discharge share `flame-mask-view.js`. Their local RGBA mask uses `flameMaskRect(shape)` and the returned `flameFogMask` bytes. Both shaders receive the integer world origin and texture dimensions, with UVs `(floor(world + .5) - origin + .5) / size`. Same-sized masks reuse storage and texture; resizing releases the previous texture. No sector-sized fire texture or legacy 40×30 coordinate assumption remains.

Renderer tests cover 200 window visits, exact six/three/three reuse, no additional asset requests, fog-gated props/walls, two-metre walls, saved door revisits, bounded actor reuse, far-cell minimaps, real camera-ray ground corners, late asset retirement and flame holes beyond the old map bounds. Browser review uses the actual game and a separate disposable camera/survey fixture; the fixture is not shipped.
